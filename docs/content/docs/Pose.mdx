---
title: Pose
description: A bone transform at a keyframe, with an optional weight.
---

`Pose` describes where one bone should be at a keyframe. A keyframe can hold several poses, and a pose can hold sub-poses when a rig needs a hierarchy of transforms.

`Pose` inherits from `Instance`.

## Getting started

```lua
local pose = Instance.new("Pose")
pose.Name = "LeftArm"
pose.CFrame = CFrame.Angles(math.rad(45), 0, 0)
pose.Weight = 1
keyframe:AddPose(pose)
```

The pose's `Name` must match the target bone's name in the rig. If no bone has that name, the pose is compiled but has nothing to affect.

## Properties

| Property | Type | Access | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `CFrame` | `CFrame` | Read/write | Identity | Bone transform for this pose. |
| `Weight` | `number` | Read/write | `1` | Blend weight. |

`Weight` is stored as the value you assign, including values outside `0`-`1`. When the pose is compiled, the weight is clamped to `0`-`1`. A weight of `0` contributes nothing; `1` applies the transform fully.

```lua
pose.Weight = 0.5
```

## Methods

### GetSubPoses

```lua
GetSubPoses(): {Pose}
```

Returns the pose's direct `Pose` children in a one-based table. Sub-poses let you express a chain beneath a single named pose.

### AddSubPose

```lua
AddSubPose(pose: Pose): ()
```

Parents an existing `Pose` to this pose. Passing a non-`Pose` instance raises an error.

```lua
local finger = Instance.new("Pose")
finger.Name = "LeftIndex"
pose:AddSubPose(finger)
```

### RemoveSubPose

```lua
RemoveSubPose(pose: Pose): ()
```

Unparents a sub-pose that belongs to this pose. Other instances and poses from elsewhere are ignored.

## How poses are compiled

Poses are collected recursively. A pose's `Name` selects the bone, `CFrame` supplies the transform, and `Weight` controls how much of the transform is applied. Sub-poses are compiled under their parent and can target deeper bones.

A pose under a keyframe is sampled at that keyframe's `Time`. Between keyframes, the animator interpolates the bone transforms.

## Notes

- `CFrame` is relative to the bone, not to the world.
- Poses are matched by name, so renaming a bone in the rig without renaming the pose breaks the link.
- Cloning copies the pose's `CFrame`, `Weight`, and descendants.
- Editing a pose after loading an animation track doesn't change the loaded clip.
