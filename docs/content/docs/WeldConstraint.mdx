---
title: WeldConstraint
description: Rigidly connect two parts so they move as one assembly.
---

`WeldConstraint` fixes two parts together at their current relative position. Unlike `Weld`, it uses whole parts rather than attachments.

## Getting started

```lua
local weld = Instance.new("WeldConstraint")
weld.Part0 = hull
weld.Part1 = turret
weld.Parent = hull
```

## Properties

| Property | Type | Access | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Part0` | `Part?` | Read/write | `nil` | First part. |
| `Part1` | `Part?` | Read/write | `nil` | Second part. |
| `Enabled` | `boolean` | Read/write | `true` | Whether the weld is active. |
| `Active` | `boolean` | Read-only | `true` | Whether the weld is currently simulating. |

`Part0` and `Part1` accept only `Part` instances or `nil`. Assigning another instance type is ignored. The joint is created from the parts' positions when both are set and present in the same world.

## How it behaves

The weld keeps the relative transform the parts had when the joint was created. Moving a welded part moves the assembly unless the other part is anchored.

Changing `Part0`, `Part1`, or `Enabled` releases the existing joint and rebuilds it on the next step. Both parts must belong to the same physics world; a constraint with parts in different places stays inactive.

## Notes

- Anchored parts can be welded to moving parts; the anchored side stays fixed.
- The weld doesn't merge the parts into one instance; they remain separate objects.
- Cloning copies the part references.
- A destroyed part clears its property and releases the weld.
